How to play

Controls

WASD moves. When the meter pops up, tap J (set) or K (kick) on green. At the net, L blocks their attack as it crosses. The arrow keys are optional aiming. You never pick the body part — the game uses foot, knee, chest or head depending on how high the ball is when you touch it.

The court

Official ISTAF court. You stay on your half; the LED boards are the outer wall. The glow ring at your feet shows where you are: team colour in court, gold in the free zone, blue in your service circle.

3 touches

You get up to 3 touches before the ball must go over — and you can take all three yourself. Sending it straight back with K is safe but slow and loopy. J pops the ball up HIGH and out into open space (← → pick the side) — move under it. Passing to yourself (J, J) lets you hit flatter and harder, and a spike (jump near the net, K at the top) is the fastest shot in the game — or jump exactly the same way and tap J for a feint: a soft chip that drops just over the net while they wait deep for the spike. Hard balls are hard to kick cleanly: control them with J first — a J that digs a smash pops the ball way up. Jump kicks work on any touch: off your own set it’s a spike, straight off their clear it’s a smash, otherwise it’s a high, soft clear they can easily return.

Teams (2v1 / 2v2 / 3v3): your side still gets 3 touches between you — e.g. one controls, the other spikes. Your teammate is light blue. The serve passes to the other team every 3 points, and each time your team gets it back the next teammate serves. 3v3 plays on its own venue — a bigger court out on the Lotus Pond: a server at the back and two feeders at the front left and right.

Defending

When they spike or smash, a red mark shows where it will land. Get into it (it turns green) and a dig meter appears — tap J on green and the ball pops straight up over you like a set; yellow still digs it, just lower and scrappier. Careful: if they jump but only chip it softly, that’s a feint — no dig zone appears, so sprint in toward the net. Way off (or no tap) is their point.

At the net you can block instead: jump and tap L as their attack crosses over you. A perfect block stuffs it straight back down. Everyone sees BLOCKED!.

Reach

You can play the ball when it is within about a metre of you. The touch is judged by height: low balls on the inside of the foot give the cleanest control. A spike needs you airborne, close to the net (within ~3 m) and the ball above ~1.7 m.

Timing

As the ball comes into your reach a timing meter pops up above your player by itself — just tap J or K when the needle is in the green. Sometimes the needle accelerates into the green to catch you out. Your grade — Perfect, Good, Early or Late — pops up above your head. If the ball hits you and you didn’t press anything, it bounces off your body and still counts as a touch.

Aiming

Markers

Every marker means one thing. While a ball is in the air, a dashed ring shows exactly where it will land — it shrinks as the ball comes down. The ball’s outline uses the same colours. Their red diamond only appears while they are about to hit; your blue diamond is always your own aim.

Serving

When it’s your serve you’re placed in your service circle automatically and held there until you kick. J tosses; as it drops the meter appears — tap on green. J, J = Snap serve: flat and quick, and the receiver’s green comes early. J, K = Hang serve: high and floaty, and the receiver’s green comes late. Read it as a receiver: snap has thin white streaks and a crisp “tsk”; hang leaves warm smoke and a low “whomp”; the meter says which, and a thin white tick shows where green normally is. Arrow keys aim if you want. Serve passes to the other player every 3 points; tossing and not kicking loses the point. In practice, press J any time to go serve.
